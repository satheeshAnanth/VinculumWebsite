The founding story

Three classmates. Twenty-five years. Two successful companies.

The story behind Vinculum's advisor network starts in an engineering college in Coimbatore — and runs through two companies, one NASDAQ acquisition, a $1.4B valuation, and a shared conviction about what the next generation of tech founders needs most.

Late 1990s · Coimbatore → Bengaluru

Three engineers, one blank page

Baskar Subramanian, Srividhya Srinivasan, and Srinivasan (Srini) KA were classmates at Government College of Technology, Coimbatore. All three went on to start their careers as software engineers at Texas Instruments. Then, in their twenties — with no venture capital, no playbook, and no network — they quit and started building. The company was called Impulsesoft.

1999–2005 · Impulsesoft

First company: build, win, sell

Impulsesoft built a Bluetooth audio technology stack at a time when Bluetooth was barely a standard. They made some of the earliest wireless audio headsets and embedded audio controllers — years ahead of the market. The company became a category leader and in 2005 was acquired by SiRF Technology, a NASDAQ-listed semiconductor company. The three founders spent 18 months integrating the acquisition before stepping away to ask: what's next?

2008 → Present · Amagi

Second company: harder problem, bigger canvas

In 2008, the same three founders co-founded Amagi — a media technology platform for cloud broadcasting and targeted TV advertising. This time they had experience. They still made expensive mistakes. Over 15 years they grew Amagi from a geo-targeted TV ad startup in India to a global SaaS platform serving 800+ content brands across 150 countries. Amagi is now valued at over $1.4 billion. All three are still running it.

Now · Vinculum

The question that led to this venture studio

At some point in building Amagi, you accumulate enough pattern recognition that it feels wasteful to keep it to yourself. The question the three founders started asking wasn't "how do we scale further?" — they're doing that at Amagi. It was: "How do we stop watching promising founders make the same expensive mistakes we made, when we could just tell them what we know?" Vinculum is that answer.

"The most valuable thing about our first exit wasn't the money — it was learning that the hard things in company-building are almost never the technical ones."

— Amagi founding team

What we bring

The lessons that only cost you a company to learn

From Impulsesoft's category creation and exit to Amagi's 15-year enterprise journey — these are the patterns the founding team has lived through, and what Vinculum systematically applies with every portfolio founder.

01

Being early is a strategy, not an accident

Impulsesoft built Bluetooth audio stacks years before the market arrived. Being early meant building buyer education into the GTM — and having the stamina to wait. Vinculum helps founders distinguish between "early" (survivable) and "wrong" (stop now).

Drawn from: Impulsesoft 1999–2005

02

Enterprise sales requires a different kind of patience

At Amagi, the team spent years selling to people who loved the product but couldn't sign. The economic buyer in enterprise is rarely the person in the room. We now help founders identify that person in week one, not month six.

Drawn from: Amagi enterprise GTM

03

Category creation needs proof points, not just vision

When Amagi started, cloud broadcasting didn't exist as a category. It took years of reference customers, standards engagement, and ecosystem building to shift the market. Vinculum helps founders sequence proof-point acquisition so their category narrative lands with buyers and investors simultaneously.

Drawn from: Amagi cloud broadcast category

04

A NASDAQ acquirer looks at different things than a VC

The Impulsesoft exit to SiRF was driven by IP and team quality, not ARR. Amagi's institutional rounds were driven by retention and expansion revenue. Understanding what each type of capital partner is actually buying is something most founders figure out too late. We help them understand it before they start fundraising.

Drawn from: Both Impulsesoft exit and Amagi fundraising

05

Global from day one costs less than retrofitting

Amagi is now in 150 countries. Many of the structural decisions made in the early India-only phase had to be expensively re-made when international customers arrived. Vinculum now bakes global design decisions into product and GTM conversations from the very first sprint.

Drawn from: Amagi international expansion

06

Compliance is a moat, not an overhead

In broadcast and media tech, early investment in compliance — broadcast standards, data privacy, ad tech regulation — became a barrier to entry for competitors. The founders now actively help Vinculum portfolio companies in AI and defense treat compliance as a competitive asset from day one.

Drawn from: Amagi broadcast compliance & Vinculum defense/AI thesis
